## Week 1 - History
- One writing system can be used by many languages
- Cuneiform looks weird
- Lecture Summaries: Writing developed to help people record and preserve information. Some early known writing comes from Sumerian clay tablets in Uruk, Mesopotamia, used around 3000 BCE for records such as deliveries of grain and beer. Mesopotamia means “between the rivers,” referring to the Tigris and Euphrates. Cuneiform writing was pressed into clay with a stylus; pressing wedge-shaped marks was easier than dragging lines. This shows how the material used to store information can affect how it is recorded.

## Week 3 - Markup Languages
- Structure of a text file
- Structured data, JSON and XML formats
- Lecture Summaries: Week 3 introduced markup languages, which use symbols or instructions to show how text should be formatted or interpreted. The lecture focused on Markdown, a simple, readable way to create formatted documents such as headings, paragraphs, and lists. A # creates a heading, while a blank line separates paragraphs; ordinary line breaks may be ignored so text can reflow to fit different screen sizes. The instructor also reviewed quiz and lab deadlines and explained that Markdown would be used in the lab.

## Week 4 - Keyboards & Editors
- History and structure of written text and numbers
- Markup languagess
- Lecture Summaries: Week 4 focused on text editors and finding or changing text. The lecture covered searching within a document, case-sensitive searches, matching whole words, and using search-and-replace. It also introduced wildcards, such as * to stand for any text in a filename, and regular expressions (regex) for describing more specific search patterns, such as a word followed by digits. The instructor said you don’t need to memorize regex syntax yet—just know it’s used to search for patterns in text.
